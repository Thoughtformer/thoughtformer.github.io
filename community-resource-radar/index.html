Community Resource Radar

A free, account-free web app that helps people find food, shelter, healthcare, and other local services and take the next step.

Open the live app Back to my work

Experience design

From a category to a next step.

Someone looking for a food pantry may have to work through several unrelated menus, reenter their ZIP code, and find a phone number or address on a separate page. I designed Radar around what that person needs to do next.

People can enter a ZIP code or city, choose an area on the map, set a distance, and browse by category. If a city name is shared, the app offers state-labeled choices. If someone starts with a category, a short prompt lets them enter a location or jump back to the map.

Listings highlight useful next steps when the source provides them. On a phone, a tap can open the dialer. Website and directions links are available where supported, and several provider finders can open with the selected ZIP already filled in.

Sources and coverage

Local searches with a visible source.

The live app includes HRSA health-center listings nationwide, curated San Francisco listings, and live pantry searches from the Tarrant Area Food Bank and North Texas Food Bank. Other categories and locations can lead to provider directories. Coverage varies by location and category.

Radar uses U.S. Census place and ZIP-area data to locate searches. It runs as a static web app on Cloudflare Pages, with browser-side filtering and no Radar login. Supported browsers can also install it from the web.

Providers determine their own hours, availability, and eligibility. The app includes source details so people can confirm those facts before visiting or applying.

Role and development

Design, iteration, and verification.

I set the product direction and priorities, chose the search and navigation workflows, reviewed the interface, and guided revisions through desktop and mobile testing. I used AI assistance for implementation and documentation.

Recent browser checks covered ZIP entry, city disambiguation, map selection, and the category prompt at desktop and mobile widths. The app is live and still in development. The source repository is private.
